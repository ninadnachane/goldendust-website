"When should we come?" is often the first thing people ask us, and we always answer with a question back: what do you most want to see?

If it's the wildebeest crossing a river full of crocodiles, there's a fairly narrow window. If it's lions and cheetahs hunting newborn calves, it's a different window, cheaper and quieter. If it's simply lots of animals without lots of other people, there's a third answer. The best time to visit Tanzania depends on which of those you are, so here's how the year actually works on the ground.

## The four Tanzania safari seasons

Tanzania roughly has two dry seasons and two wet ones.

### June to October: the long dry season

This is the classic safari season, and most guides will tell you it's the most reliable. The grass dries out and shortens, waterholes shrink, and animals gather near the water, which makes them much easier to find. Lions lying by a shrinking waterhole. Elephant herds heading to the Tarangire River. Leopards in acacia trees, actually visible for once.

It's also when the Great Migration reaches the northern Serengeti and the river crossings happen. Watching thousands of wildebeest hesitate on the bank and then plunge in is something you don't forget.

The catch: it's peak season. Prices are highest, the best camps fill months ahead, and the popular sightings get busy.

### January to March: the short dry season

This is the window we'd pick, and it's underrated. The herds are in the southern Serengeti for calving, thousands of calves born within a few weeks of each other. Lions, cheetahs, hyenas and wild dogs follow them, and the predator action is intense, raw and hard to watch at times.

The plains are still green from the short rains, there are noticeably fewer visitors, and prices drop. If your dates are flexible, look hard at these months.

### April and May: the long rains

These months have a bad reputation, and some of it is earned. The rain can be heavy and last for hours, some camps close, and the tracks turn to mud. But the parks are nearly empty, prices are the lowest of the year, and the birdlife is excellent. Not ideal for a first safari, but some travelers prefer it.

### November and December: the short rains

The short rains are lighter and patchier. You might get a hard shower in the afternoon and clear skies the rest of the day. The Serengeti turns green almost overnight, migratory birds arrive from the north, and the game viewing is good. Fewer visitors and middle-of-the-range prices make this a real option.

## Month by month

**January–March:** Go to the southern Serengeti for calving and predators. Warm, sometimes cloudy, rarely disrupted by rain.

**April–May:** Long rains, rough roads, some lodges closed. Cheap, quiet and great for birders, but not our first choice for a first trip.

**June–July:** The dry season starts concentrating the animals. The [Great Migration in Tanzania](/) moves north towards the Grumeti River. Conditions get better across the Serengeti, Tarangire and Ngorongoro.

**August–October:** The peak. River crossings in the northern Serengeti, dry and cool weather, full camps and the highest prices. Book early.

**November–December:** Short rains freshen everything up. Resident wildlife is active, bird numbers rise and the parks are much quieter than they'll be in a few months. Good value.

## Which parks, when

**Serengeti National Park** changes most with the season. The south is the place from January to March (calving), and the north from July to October (crossings). Our [Serengeti vs Ngorongoro guide](/post/serengeti-vs-ngorongoro-which-safari-experience-is-right-for-you) covers how to split your time.

**Ngorongoro Crater** works all year, because the animals live there permanently. Early morning drives in the dry season give the clearest sightings.

**Tarangire National Park** is at its best from July to October, when elephants gather along the Tarangire River in numbers you have to see to believe.

**Nyerere (Selous)** is best in the dry season, June to October, when boat trips on the Rufiji River and walking safaris are at their best.

## Budget and crowds

Peak season costs more, and there's no way around that. But some things, like the river crossings, only happen then. If budget matters, January to March and November are the sweet spot: excellent wildlife, quieter parks and lower prices. Good months for a [budget safari in Tanzania](/post/budget-safari-tanzania-chasing-the-big-five-on-a-backpacker-s-budget).

## Our honest answer

If someone just asks for the best time to visit Tanzania for a safari, we say June to October: reliable weather, animals easy to find, and the river crossings. If they ask when *we'd* go, it's January to March.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**1. What is the best time to visit Tanzania for the wildebeest migration?**

For the river crossings, July to September, when the herds cross the Mara River in the northern Serengeti. For calving and predator action, January to March in the southern Serengeti.

**2. Is Tanzania safe to visit during the rainy season?**

Yes. The rains are a logistics issue, not a safety one: muddy tracks, some camps closed and the odd afternoon downpour. Most parks stay open, and some travelers love how quiet it is.

**3. How far ahead should I book a Tanzania safari?**

For July to October, six to twelve months ahead. The good Serengeti camps fill early, and last-minute options in peak season are few and expensive.

**4. Can I visit Tanzania's national parks in December?**

Yes, and it's a good month. The short rains are usually light, the parks are quiet and green, and prices sit below peak season.

**5. Do Tanzania safari seasons affect visa requirements?**

No. Entry rules are the same all year. Most nationalities can get a visa on arrival or apply online through the e-visa portal before they travel.
