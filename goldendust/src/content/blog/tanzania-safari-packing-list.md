Most guests over-pack. On safari you spend your days in the vehicle and your evenings at camp, so you need less than you think. Here's what our guides tell guests before they arrive.

## Clothes

- **Neutral colours.** Khaki, beige, olive and brown. Dark blue and black attract tsetse flies, and white shows every bit of dust.
- **Layers.** Mornings start cool, middays are warm. A T-shirt, a long-sleeved shirt and a fleece cover most days.
- **A warm jacket.** The Ngorongoro Crater rim is over 2,000 m high and the nights are cold all year.
- **Long sleeves and trousers** for the evening, to keep mosquitoes off.
- **Comfortable shoes.** Trainers are fine. You won't walk much on game drives.
- **A sun hat and a light rain jacket**, especially from November to May.
- **Swimwear**, if your lodge has a pool.

Many camps offer laundry, so three or four days of clothes is usually enough.

## For the game drives

- **Binoculars.** One pair each if you can. This is the thing guests most often wish they'd brought.
- **Camera** with a zoom lens, spare batteries and memory cards. Most camps have somewhere to charge.
- **Sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm.** The sun is strong near the equator.
- **A scarf or buff** for dusty roads.

## Health and toiletries

- Any medicines you take, in your hand luggage.
- Insect repellent and antimalarials if your doctor recommends them. See our [vaccinations guide](/post/yellow-fever-vaccination-tanzania-safari).
- Hand sanitiser and tissues.

## Documents

- Passport, valid for at least six months, and your [visa](/post/tanzania-visa-guide-for-safari-travellers).
- A yellow fever certificate if your route needs one.
- Travel insurance details.
- Some US dollars in small, recent notes for tips and extras.

## Bags

Use a **soft bag** rather than a hard suitcase. It fits better in the vehicle, and if you take a small plane to or from the Serengeti, airlines usually require soft bags and set a weight limit of around 15 kg. Check with your airline.

## Leave at home

- **Plastic carrier bags.** They're banned in Tanzania and can be taken from you on arrival.
- Camouflage clothing, which in Tanzania is for the military.
- Too many smart clothes. Safari camps are relaxed.

## Plugs

Most sockets in Tanzania take UK-style three-pin plugs (type G). Bring an adapter.
